## The eight topics (authored)

1. **Vibe:** credible, warm, ambitious. "An attorney who sat at your kitchen table." References: a Black Enterprise cover, a federal grant form, the hourglass in her own logo.
2. **Journey:** who she is and what she promises, why grants feel out of reach, why she is the right guide, the path (Start Here), what it costs (consultations and books), one action.
3. **Energy:** a confident open, quiet in the middle where trust is built, rising into the action.
4. **Feeling and the one moment:** see each concept's curve below. One peak each.
5. **Something no site does:** each concept has its own signature move (below).
6. **Range:** three different families on purpose: editorial (A), dense and utilitarian (B), premium dark (C).
7. **One world or distinct scenes:** distinct scenes in all three. No continuous worldflight.
8. **Assets:** her real portraits and logo. No generated imagery. Book covers are typeset in the page instead of using her low-res cover mockups.

**What the visitor must believe by the end:** winning a grant is a learnable process, and Ebonie will walk you through it.
**One action, one label:** "Get the checklist" (the free Business Start-Up Checklist). Secondary links go to Book a session and Shop.

## Concept A: The Cover (grammar: chaptered editorial)

A business-magazine cover where Ebonie is the cover star, then a printed feature in five chapters.

Feeling curve:
1. Pride: a magazine cover with her standing in front of the masthead, head over the letters.
2. Recognition: Chapter I names the problem in plain type on paper (the money exists, the process is opaque).
3. Trust: Chapter II, Walnut Park to the bar, her quote set large, credentials as plain fact.
4. Clarity: Chapter III, the four Start Here steps as a numbered running list (a real sequence).
5. Appetite: Chapter IV, the two books typeset as objects with real prices.
6. Resolve: the colophon. The CTA as a line of running text.

**Peak:** "The cover lines flew off the cover and became the table of contents." (Act 1 to 2.)
**Tell someone:** "It's the site where she's on the cover of her own business magazine."
**Signature move:** the cover lines are the navigation. As you scroll off the cover, each cover line travels to the margin folio and becomes a live chapter link.

## Concept B: The Application (grammar: live surface)

The homepage is a grant application, and it fills itself in as you scroll. Copy lives in the idiom of the form: field labels, helper text, checkboxes, a budget worksheet.

Feeling curve:
1. Familiar dread: a blank grant application, the thing everybody puts off.
2. Relief: Section A is already filled in, with Ebonie as the applicant and her photo clipped to the corner.
3. Confidence: qualifications check themselves off.
4. Control: the visitor builds a real budget from her real consultation prices and the total updates.
5. Readiness: the visitor types their business name and watches it stamped onto the approval seal.

**Peak:** "I typed my business name and it got stamped APPROVED FOR NEXT STEPS." (The close.)
**Tell someone:** "It's the site where the homepage is a grant application that fills itself in."
**Signature move:** a live seal. The applicant's own business name is set into an embossed seal in real time, and the status bar's completeness meter is driven by how far they read.

## Concept C: Guessing vs. Money Time (grammar: split stage)

Two columns held in tension for the whole page: guessing your way to funding on the left, the Money Time way on the right. The divider is an hourglass.

Feeling curve:
1. Tension: both columns visible at once. The cold side and the warm side.
2. Recognition: the guessing side names what they are doing now.
3. Contrast: each row, the right side answers with a real Money Time offer.
4. Momentum: sand visibly moves from the guessing side's bulb to Money Time's.
5. Release: the hourglass flips, the divider sweeps to the edge, Money Time takes the whole screen.

**Peak:** "The hourglass emptied, flipped, and the whole page turned gold." (The collapse.)
**Tell someone:** "It's the site where the hourglass from her logo runs out on guessing."
**Signature move:** the divider is a working hourglass. Scroll pours the sand, and its last grain triggers the collapse.
